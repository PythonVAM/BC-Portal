Recommendation

Option 3 — BC Leads + Contributors + Optional FBP approval, with the approval made threshold-mandatory, not discretionary.

It's the option closest to what's already been built, and it fixes the one genuine control gap in that direction: the offline, un-auditable sign-off becomes an in-system step. Since this isn't a SOX process, the target is proportionate control — so make the FBP approval mandatory above a value / materiality threshold (small changes flow through; material ones always get an independent second pair of eyes), and auto-notify the SET BC Lead by rule. This gives a defensible four-eyes control at the lowest rework.

What would change this: if FBPs already drive most BCs in practice, option 4 (FBPs as BC Leads) stops being a big change and its stronger controls make it the better pick. The deciding question is empirical — today, who actually drives BCs: the FP&A lead or the FBP?

Best-practice assessment

This is not a SOX-controlled process, so controls should be proportionate to the risk and materiality of each boundary change rather than driven by external compliance. Even so, the same widely-used governance principles apply as good practice:

Segregation of duties (COSO / ISACA) Four-eyes / maker–checker Auditability & record-keeping Clear accountability (RACI) Consulted / Informed by rule Systematic over discretionary controls Proportionate / risk-based control Adoption & change management

The recommendation is at the top of this page; each option's own page carries a per-option best-practice verdict. Ratings reflect general governance principles applied to this process — the precise bar would be set by your own risk appetite and materiality thresholds.
