Best-practice assessment

This is not a SOX-controlled process, so controls should be proportionate to the risk and materiality of each boundary change rather than driven by external compliance. Even so, the same widely-used governance principles apply as good practice:

Segregation of duties (COSO / ISACA) Four-eyes / maker–checker Auditability & record-keeping Clear accountability (RACI) Consulted / Informed by rule Systematic over discretionary controls Proportionate / risk-based control Adoption & change management

Headline

  • Strongest control alignment — FBP-led. A mandatory, in-system, two-level approval (FBP leads, SET BC Lead approves) with clean segregation of duties and a full audit trail.
  • Best fit to current ways of working — Fluid and Fluid · Consulted. They mirror how teams already operate, at the cost of control guarantees.
  • Weakest control points. The Proposed option's offline, un-auditable sign-off; and the fluid options' lack of a mandatory independent approver plus oversight that relies on complex logic (Fluid) or the Lead's memory (Consulted) — a discretionary control, which best practice disfavours.

Recommendation (proportionate, since not SOX)

Take a flexible base but harden three things: (1) make the independent approval mandatory above a value / risk threshold rather than optional or absent; (2) keep every approval in-system for a clean trail; and (3) auto-notify the SET BC Lead for their area by rule, not by discretion. In practice that's the Optional-FBP step made threshold-mandatory, layered on a fluid model, with a systematic “Informed” rule — capturing most of the adoption upside without the control gaps.

Per-option verdict

  • Proposed — simple with clear ownership; the weak link is the offline approval.
  • Optional FBP Approval — right idea, but discretionary; make it threshold-mandatory.
  • FBP-led — best controls; main cost is the change impact on some areas.
  • Fluid roles — best adoption; needs a mandatory checker and systematic notifications.
  • Fluid · Consulted — flexible and simpler, but discretionary notification is the control weakness.

Ratings reflect general governance principles applied to this process; the precise bar would be set by your own risk appetite and materiality thresholds.
